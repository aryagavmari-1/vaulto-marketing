Before you type in your mortgage balance, your life insurance policy number, or a photo of your passport, it's fair to stop and ask one question: who else is going to see this?

That's not a paranoid question — it's the right one. A family asset vault only works if you actually put things in it, and you won't put things in it if you're not sure who's on the other end. So here's a plain answer, section by section: exactly who can see what's in your vault, and who can't.

## The real question before you type anything in

Most apps answer "is it safe?" with an adjective — "bank-grade," "secure," "encrypted." None of that tells you who can actually open your account and look. The more useful question is an access-control question: **which specific people, systems, and job roles can see this data, and under what conditions?**

That's what this post walks through for Vaulto — not a marketing claim, a list. If you're also weighing the broader question of whether financial apps in general are safe to use, our [safety checklist for storing financial information in an app](/blog/is-it-safe-to-store-financial-information-in-an-app/) covers that groundwork; this post picks up the narrower question of *who has access*.

## A private vault you control

Your vault is yours. You decide what goes into it — which accounts, which documents, which valuables — and you decide who, if anyone, you bring in to help. Nobody is added to your vault without your action: there's no default sharing, no automatic connection to anyone else's account, and no way for another Vaulto user to find or open your vault.

That's the starting position everything else in this post builds on: a private vault you control, not a shared or public space.

## Your vault is isolated from other users

Vaulto is a multi-tenant app — many families use it — so the practical question is whether that separation actually holds. It does: each person's vault is isolated from other users. Your entries, values, and files sit in your vault and nowhere else; another user's vault has no path into yours, and yours has no path into theirs.

## Support cannot browse your vault

This is the one people usually assume is untrue, because with a lot of software it isn't: **support cannot browse your vault.** There is no staff-facing screen or admin tool that lets a Vaulto support person open your entries, read a document, or see a value you've recorded. If you write in with a question, whoever helps you can see account-level details needed to support you — not the contents of your vault.

## You choose, per file, whether AI reads it

Vaulto can use AI to help you get organized faster — for example, reading a bank statement or an insurance schedule and suggesting the fields to fill in. Whether that happens is a choice you make **per file**: for each document you upload, you choose to let Vaulto's AI read it and extract key fields, or to store the file only, with no AI reading it at all. That choice sits alongside a vault-level AI setting, but the per-file decision is always yours to make.

Nothing here is "off by default and you turn it on" or the reverse in a way you don't control — it's a switch you flip per document, every time you add one.

## Sharing is scoped to a capture session, not your whole vault

Plenty of families want to build their vault together — a partner adding the accounts they manage, an adult child helping an ageing parent get organized. Vaulto supports that, but it's worth being precise about what "sharing" actually means here: when you invite someone in, you're inviting them into a **capture session** — a bounded space for building out part of your inventory together — with a defined role: capturer, reviewer, or viewer.

That's different from handing over the whole vault. It isn't co-ownership of everything you've recorded, and it isn't an automatic executor or beneficiary handoff if something happens to you — those are separate questions about legacy access, worth thinking through on their own terms, not something a shared session grants by accident.

## How your data is kept

Separately from who can see it, there's the question of how it's stored: your records sit encrypted at rest, using managed encryption keys, on cloud infrastructure — which also means you can open your vault from your phone or laptop, wherever you are, rather than being tied to one device or location. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is my information private in an asset inventory app?**
It depends on the app, so ask before you trust one with your family's financial details. In Vaulto, your vault is a private vault you control: each person's vault is isolated from other users, and our support team cannot browse it. The only other people who see anything are the ones you specifically invite into a capture session, and even then only what that session covers — not your whole vault.

**Can app staff see my financial data?**
With some tools, yes — support staff can open your account to help you. With Vaulto, support cannot browse your vault: there is no staff-facing screen that lets a support person read your entries, values, or files. If you contact support with a question, they can see account and billing details needed to help you, not the contents of your vault.

**Who has access to my family finance data in Vaulto?**
By default, only you. Two things can extend that, both under your control: inviting someone into a capture session (scoped to that session, with a role — capturer, reviewer, or viewer — not full access to everything), and choosing, per file, whether Vaulto's AI reads it to extract key fields. Nobody else — not other users, not support — can browse your vault.

## What this means for your family

Put together, the access list is short: you, by default. Anyone you specifically invite, scoped to a capture session and a role you choose. Vaulto's AI, only for the files you allow it to read. Not other users. Not support, browsing at will. That's a private vault you control — worth knowing in plain terms before you list what your family actually owns.
