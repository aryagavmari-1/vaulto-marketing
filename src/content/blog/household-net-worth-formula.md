Household net worth is one honest number: everything your household owns, minus everything it owes. Work it out once and you have a starting point; keep it current and you have a way to watch your family's financial standing move in the right direction over time.

This guide gives you the formula, a short worked example, and the two lists you need to build it — then how to stop it going stale in a forgotten spreadsheet.

## The household net worth formula

There's only one formula, and it's short:

> **Household net worth = total assets − total liabilities**
>
> (what you own − what you owe)

That's the whole calculation. The effort isn't the maths — it's gathering an honest list of each side. Once you have both totals, a single subtraction gives you the number. If you want the broader, emotional picture of what this means for your family, our [full guide to your family's net worth](/blog/how-to-calculate-your-familys-net-worth/) covers the *why*; this piece is the *how*, with a worked example you can copy.

A quick note on what net worth is **not**: it isn't your monthly budget (that's cash flow), it isn't a credit score, and a small or negative number isn't a verdict. It's a snapshot — a place to start.

## What counts as an asset

Assets are everything of value your household holds. Walk these categories and write down a figure, or a value-*range* where you're unsure:

- **Property** — your main home plus any second property or land, entered at what it would realistically sell for today rather than what you paid.
- **Vehicles** — anything with a logbook you could sell on: the family car, a motorbike, a caravan or a boat, priced at what a buyer would hand over now.
- **Cash** — the money you can reach quickly: current-account balances, savings pots, and whatever's set aside as an emergency fund.
- **Investments** — money working for later: workplace and private pensions, ISAs, shares, and funds, taken from their most recent statement value.
- **Valuables** — the smaller-but-real items you'd insure: jewellery, watches, art, or a collection you'd claim for if it went missing.

Don't stall chasing an exact number. A sensible estimate — or a "£3,000–£5,000" range — is far more useful than a blank.

## What counts as a liability

Liabilities are everything your household owes. The other side of the ledger:

- **Mortgage** — the amount still left to clear on your home loan, which your latest statement will show — not the sum you first borrowed.
- **Loans** — any fixed borrowing you're still paying down: a car-finance deal, a personal loan, or a remaining student-loan balance.
- **Credit cards** — the part of each balance that rolls over month to month, rather than anything you clear in full.
- **Other debts** — anything else with your name on it: an overdraft, a buy-now-pay-later plan, or money owed to family.

Listing what you owe isn't discouraging — it's what makes the final number *true*. A list of assets with no liabilities is a wish, not a net worth.

## How to calculate household net worth, step by step

1. **List every asset with a value (or value-range).** Use the categories above. Rough is fine; you can refine later.
2. **List every liability with its current balance.** Statements or your banking app will have the up-to-date figures.
3. **Add each side, then subtract.** Total assets minus total liabilities is your household net worth.

### A worked example

Here's a synthetic household to show the shape of it. Your numbers will differ — the method won't.

| Assets | Value | Liabilities | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | £420,000 | Mortgage | £268,000 |
| Car | £11,000 | Car loan | £6,500 |
| Cash & savings | £18,500 | Credit cards | £2,800 |
| Pensions & investments | £96,000 | Student loan | £9,700 |
| Valuables | £4,500 | | |
| **Total assets** | **£550,000** | **Total liabilities** | **£287,000** |

> **Net worth = £550,000 − £287,000 = £263,000**

That £263,000 is the cushion this household actually stands on. Positive is a buffer; negative simply tells you precisely where you are — which is the only place real progress starts.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I include in household net worth?

Everything of value your household owns and everything it owes. On the asset side: property, vehicles, cash and savings, pensions and investments, and valuables like jewellery or art. On the liability side: your mortgage, loans, credit-card balances, and any other debts. Use a realistic current value — or a value-range — for anything you can't pin down exactly.

### How often should I calculate it?

For most families, quarterly is plenty, plus a fresh look after any big change — a house move, a new loan, a large gift or inheritance, or a job change. The point of a regular rhythm isn't precision to the penny; it's keeping the number honest enough to trust when you make a decision.

### Is my home equity part of net worth?

Yes. Your home counts as an asset at its current market value, and your outstanding mortgage counts as a liability. The difference between them — your home equity — is what actually flows into net worth, rather than the full value of the property.

### Net worth vs. income — what's the difference?

Income is what you earn over a period; net worth is what you're left holding at a point in time. A high income doesn't guarantee a high net worth if it all goes out again, and a modest income can build a healthy net worth over years. Net worth is the truer measure of financial standing because it counts what you've kept, not just what you've made.
