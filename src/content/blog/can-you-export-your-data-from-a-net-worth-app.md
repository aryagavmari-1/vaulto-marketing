If you're weighing whether to put your family's financial records into an app, one question tends to come before all the others: *what if I want to leave?* It's a fair thing to ask before you spend an afternoon typing in account details, property values and insurance policies. You shouldn't have to trust a product with no exit.

So here's a direct answer, using Vaulto as the example, since it's the tool this is about: what you can export, what it doesn't mean, and why the question matters regardless of which app you end up choosing.

## You own what you put in

The records in your vault — the accounts you've listed, the property values you've entered, the notes you've written for whoever needs them — are yours. You typed them in; nothing about putting them into an app should mean you've lost the ability to take them back out, print them, hand them to a solicitor, or move them somewhere else entirely. A tool that makes that hard, or that hides the option three settings menus deep, is telling you something about how it sees your relationship with your own data.

## What you can actually export

In Settings, you can export your vault's records as **JSON, CSV or PDF** — whichever format suits what you're doing with them, whether that's a spreadsheet for your own reference or a document to hand to an executor. Beyond individual records, you can also download a **full archive as a ZIP file**, and this is the part worth being specific about: it includes not just your typed data, but the **original photos and scans you've uploaded** — a picture of a ring for insurance, a scanned deed, a PDF of a policy document. Export everything, including your files, isn't a hypothetical feature; it's what the button actually does.

That matters because a records-only export can quietly leave the most useful part behind. A CSV row that says "wedding ring, £4,000" is far less useful to an insurer or an executor than the CSV row *plus* the photo and the receipt you scanned in alongside it. A real export takes the whole picture with it.

## Stored off-site, open from anywhere

Your vault lives in the cloud rather than on a single device, which is a plain, practical point about access, not a security claim: it means you can open it from your phone, a laptop, or a family member's computer, rather than being tied to the one machine you first entered everything on. That's worth separating clearly from questions like who can see your data or how it's protected — those are real questions, but they're a different post. This one is about whether you can get your own information back out, and the answer there doesn't depend on where the data happens to sit.

## What export is not

It's worth being clear about what export *doesn't* mean, too. Vaulto doesn't connect to your bank, and it doesn't scan, sync with, or pull data from any financial institution on your behalf. So when you export your vault, you're exporting your own entries and your own uploaded files — the record you built, not a live feed of your accounts. If you're picturing something like an open banking connection that quietly ties into your accounts and could be revoked or expired, that's not what's happening here, in either direction. What goes in is what you typed or uploaded; what comes out is exactly that, cleanly.

## Why portability is a trust feature

An export option isn't a nice-to-add extra bolted onto a product late in its life — it's a signal about how a company thinks about your data in the first place. A tool that makes it easy to leave, at any point, with everything you put in, has less reason to make its interface confusing or its retention vague, because it isn't relying on friction to keep you. Portability is what turns "I'll try this and see" into a decision you can actually make, because trying it doesn't mean committing to it.

## FAQ

**Can I export my data from a net worth app?**
It depends entirely on the app, so it's worth checking before you commit anything to one. In Vaulto, yes: you can export your records as JSON, CSV or PDF at any time, or download a full archive that includes the original photos and scans you've uploaded, not just the typed details. There's no minimum time you have to use it first and no support ticket required.

**How do I avoid data lock-in with a financial or inventory app?**
Look for a plain, self-serve export option before you sign up — not a promise buried in a privacy policy, but an actual button in the app. If the only way to get your information out is to ask support and wait, that's lock-in by friction even if it's technically possible. Vaulto's export sits in Settings and produces a file you can open immediately, on your own, without contacting anyone.

**What's included when I export my vault, and what's left out?**
A full export includes every record you've entered — asset and liability details, values, notes — plus the original files you've uploaded, like photos of valuables or scanned documents. It does not include a live feed from your bank, because Vaulto doesn't connect to or pull from your bank in the first place. Export gives you back exactly what you put in: your own entries and your own files.

**Does exporting my data from Vaulto mean it's no longer safe there?**
No — export and storage are separate questions. Being able to export everything is about whether you're free to leave, not about how your data is handled while you stay. Vaulto stores your records and files off the premises in the cloud, so you can reach your vault from any device; that's a separate, ordinary fact about where the data lives, not a comment on who can see it.
